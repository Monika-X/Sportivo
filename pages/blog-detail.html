Perfecting Your Serve

Oct 24, 2026 By Sarah Jenkins 5 min read

The serve is arguably the most important shot in tennis. It is the only shot where you have complete control over the ball, the timing, and your positioning. Mastering the biomechanics of a powerful and accurate tennis serve can elevate your game from intermediate to advanced, allowing you to dictate play from the very first strike.

1. The Grip and Stance

Before you even toss the ball, your foundation must be solid. A continental grip is essential for hitting flat, slice, and kick serves interchangeably without giving away your intention to your opponent. Your stance should be relaxed, with your front foot pointing slightly towards the net post and your back foot parallel to the baseline.

"A great serve isn't just about arm speed; it's about kinetic chain transfer from the ground up." - Elite Tennis Coach

2. The Toss

The toss is the steering wheel of the serve. A consistent toss leads to a consistent serve. Practice tossing the ball straight up into the air with a straight arm, releasing the ball at about eye level. The ball should peak slightly in front of your hitting shoulder for a flat serve.

3. The Trophy Pose and Leg Drive

As you toss the ball, your body should coil into the 'trophy pose'. Your knees should bend deeply, loading energy into your legs, while your hitting arm drops behind your head (the 'racket drop'). As the ball reaches its apex, explode upwards, driving from your legs and rotating your hips and shoulders into the shot.

4. Contact and Follow-Through

Make contact with the ball at the highest comfortable point, extending your arm fully. Pronate your wrist (turn it outwards) through contact to generate maximum power and spin. Follow through across your body, landing balanced on your front foot, ready for the return.
